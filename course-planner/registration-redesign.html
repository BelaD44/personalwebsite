01 / Mission statement

Make planning and registration one continuous task.

Create a single page where UT students can browse courses, assemble a schedule, see a weekly calendar and cost estimate, and perform registration actions with clear feedback.

The working goal is a free proof of concept that reduces self-reported stress and aims to cut the time needed to complete a full registration by more than 50%. Those are project hypotheses, not measured outcomes.

Boundary of this project

The class scope is interface design with realistic test scenarios. Authentication, live course seats, tuition data, and actual enrollment require university systems and are outside this prototype.

02 / Customer needfinding

Students prepare across several tools, then act under time pressure.

The customer needs assessment records ten interviews, individual voice-of-customer notes, and a survey. The patterns below reflect those materials; they are research observations about the existing workflow, not feedback on a new prototype.

10

Interview records

Students described combining the course schedule, UT Registration Plus, notes or spreadsheets of unique numbers, and a separate calendar.

38

Survey respondents

32 completed every question. The survey charts show friction around moving schedules into calendars, finding alternatives for full classes, and tracking information across pages.

0

Manual numbers as a goal

The needs list calls for selecting courses without typing unique numbers. The survey also asked students about mistyped numbers and wrong registration actions.

What the current workflow revealed

Browse course schedule→Compare details and time slots across tabs→Save unique numbers and backups→Enter actions on registration page→Copy schedule into calendar

Interviewees described registration at home on laptops or desktops, often after planning during the prior week. One student kept unique numbers in a Word document at large type for quick copying. Others wanted conflicts and failed actions explained before the final submission. The derived needs list gives the highest weight (5/5) to reliable add, drop, conditional add/drop, batch registration, and stability during the registration window.

05 / House of Quality

The House of Quality links 15 selected customer needs to 13 engineering requirements with relationship weights of 9, 3, and 1. Its ranked priorities put legible type first, clicks to register four classes second, and batch capacity third.

06 / Idea generation and preliminary prototypes

Explore five ways to organize the same job.

The project used a mind map, 6-3-5 ideation, design by analogy, TRIZ, and five annotated interface sketches. The sketches serve as preliminary, low-fidelity prototypes that preceded the separate browser prototypes below.

01 · Three-panel dashboard

Course search, calendar, and registration cart remain visible together. Backups attach to cart items and results appear on the calendar.

02 · Calendar-first

A large weekly calendar anchors the page; course details open in a popup and a persistent bar shows the cart and estimates.

03 · Guided wizard

Students move through finding, choosing, registering, and exporting with a visible progress path.

04 · Schedule generator

The system ranks conflict-free schedules using preferences such as late starts or a free Friday.

05 · Command bar

Autocomplete, pasted unique numbers, and keyboard shortcuts favor experienced users who prioritize speed.

08 / Prototyping and user feedback

What preliminary user feedback changed

Two reviewers responded to the works-like prototype. Elisante liked the three-section structure and stacked filters, but found the first screen busy and asked for less scrolling, a simpler summary, clearer calendar tags, and better cart updates. Elia asked for full department names, an “Open classes only” filter, consistent spacing, less permanent explanatory text, and clearer links between the calendar and course list.

Design response

Keep the three-panel architecture while reducing visible clutter. Prioritize essential status, credit hours, and the registration action; move secondary explanations into optional detail. These are directions from feedback, not a claim that every request has been implemented.

The project’s needs review also identifies gaps in the proof of concept: registered classes do not immediately update the calendar, manual registration can be easy to miss, and legibility on a 13-inch laptop needs verification. No measured time savings or formal usability results are documented yet.

09 / Beta plan and future work

Test the design before connecting it to real registration.

01 · Model the beta

Use a screen-design file or equivalent CAD-style interface specification to define the component library, desktop and 13-inch laptop layouts, states, and interaction flows.

02 · Run task-based trials

Test with undergraduates and graduate students. Compare the current workflow with the prototype on a four-class registration scenario and a full-section backup scenario.

03 · Check system feasibility

Validate course data access, authentication, seat freshness, waitlist rules, accessibility, and load behavior with the registrar and university IT before any live integration.

Current outcome

A research-backed direction, with validation still ahead.

The semester work establishes the problem, prioritized needs, models, five layout concepts, and two separate browser prototypes. Preliminary reviewers support the three-panel structure while pointing to density and clarity issues. The next step is a measured usability study and beta specification.
